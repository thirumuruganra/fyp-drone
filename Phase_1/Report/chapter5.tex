\chapter{SOCIAL IMPACT AND SUSTAINABILITY}\label{cha:social}

\section{SOCIAL IMPACT}

The people who gain most from drone delivery are those far from a hospital or cut off
by an emergency: patients in rural clinics waiting for blood or antivenom, towns
isolated by a flood, and hospitals that send samples to a central laboratory every day.
Zipline's network in Rwanda~\cite{r16} and the hospital sample flights in
Switzerland~\cite{r12} show that such services already exist. What our work adds is a
way for each flight to suit its cargo. Urgent antivenom can take the shortest corridor
and a fragile sample a calmer one, without the operator knowing how the planner works.

This matters most where trained staff are scarce. The person launching a drone at a
district hospital or a relief camp is likely to be a nurse, a volunteer or a logistics
worker, not a route-planning expert. Our system asks four simple questions about the
payload (how fragile, how heavy, how urgent, how important) and handles the rest.

A delivery drone also flies over people who never agreed to it. Population exposure,
restricted zones, water and building proximity are part of every edge's cost, nodes
above the legal ceiling are removed, and a weather gate stops flights in storms, heavy
rain, poor visibility or strong gusts. It keeps the planner from routing over a crowded market to
save a few seconds.

Every Tier~2 decision is recorded with its tags, weights, route and any HOLD reason, so
it can be reproduced later. The benefit depends on honest limits, though. The fragility tag is a precaution, not a proven protection~\cite{r13,r14,r15},
and until the risk weights are calibrated, the risk numbers should be read as rankings
between routes, not as probabilities of harm. Outdoor flights in Phase~II will follow
the Drone Rules, 2021~\cite{r21}.

\section{SUSTAINABILITY}

For short, light deliveries, a small electric drone replaces a road trip by van or
motorcycle and produces no exhaust where it flies. The planner adds to this in two
ways. Its energy objective sees wind direction, which alone changed the energy of the
cheapest route by 27.5\% in our study, so it can avoid strong headwinds. And the payload
weight tag gives energy more weight when the load is heavy, which is when battery use
matters most. The feasibility filter refuses routes that would eat into the 20\%
battery reserve, which protects both the mission and the battery's life.

The system is also light on computing. Tier~1 runs on an ordinary laptop CPU with no
GPU or cloud service, once per region and weather window, and its front is reused by
every mission until it expires. Tier~2 takes a fraction of a millisecond and can run on
a Raspberry Pi on board.

Everything the system depends on is open data or open-source software, so there are no
licence fees and the method can be set up for any region. With all constants in one file
and about 265 offline tests, it can be recalibrated as real flight data comes in.
